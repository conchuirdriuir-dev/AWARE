Reviewer: fresh Claude agent, behavioural and sensory biology lens; given the design packet only.

## Summary

Read as an organism, the design is a creature with one sense (text in), one effector (text out), a fixed nervous system (frozen weights) and a homeostatic setpoint (the frozen origin). Its observer resembles a crude reafference system. The biggest gap is that provenance is tagged by authorship ("who wrote this chunk"), whereas biology tags by causation ("would this sensation have happened without my action?"). That is the distinction von Holst and Mittelstaedt's reafference principle and Sperry's corollary discharge exist to make. Most of the compass's hard cases turn on that distinction: a captured partner, a tool result that merely confirms a successful action, a probe response, a re-inserted origin. The design currently separates them only by how much news arrives. Three conflations follow. First, a single pooled gain mixes taking up information with rejecting disturbance, and the stated goal ("pursue goals over perturbations") requires low gain to perturbations. Second, "gain at zero" lumps together habituation, sensory adaptation, satiation and effector limits, which biology tells apart with cheap tests (stimulus specificity, dishabituation, spontaneous recovery). Third, the compass has no reading for gain that is too high: the entity captured by its partner, the mirror image of the capture it does detect. Smaller findings concern stereotypies that respond briefly and then return to their cycle, the single 21-state memory horizon, an absolute probe threshold where foraging theory prescribes a relative one, an orienting-response bias in the opening gain reference, and a toy environment that never reacts to the agent. Every finding below ends in a change to what is measured.

## Findings (ranked by how much they would change the design)

### 1. Provenance must be causal (reafference), not authorial; capture is a contingency, not a low news level

**Basis.** Von Holst and Mittelstaedt (1950) and Sperry (1950): the nervous system subtracts the predicted sensory consequence of its own action (efference copy, corollary discharge) and treats only the residual as the world (exafference). The source of the content is irrelevant. What matters is whether the sensation depends on the act. Mormyrid electric fish learn a "negative image" of their own discharge's reafference in the electrosensory lobe (Bell 1981; Requarth and Sawtell 2011), and prey show up as the residual. Crickets suppress auditory input during their own chirps (Poulet and Hedwig 2006). Animals detect mirrors the same way, by contingency testing: they make unusual movements and check whether the "other" matches them. This is the first stage of mirror self-recognition in apes, magpies and cleaner wrasse (Kohda et al. 2019), and infants use contingency detection the same way (Watson). A captured partner is a mirror.

In this design, a tool result confirming a successful action, a test that passes, the partner's answer to a probe, and the re-inserted origin are all "external" by origin but reafferent by cause. Under the current definition, a confirmed action yields low news: the arrival matches what recent history contained. The compass then reads "news at zero → partner captured" for what is actually healthy closed-loop motor control. Meanwhile "news at zero" (capture) and "news low, gain intact" (dull world) are told apart only by a news threshold, a difference of degree for what is a difference in kind.

**Change in measurement.**
- (a) Record two fields per chunk: *content origin* (self/external, as now) and *arrival cause* (spontaneous vs elicited by the entity's preceding action: tool call, probe, correction).
- (b) Fit a forward model, the efference-copy prediction: predict each elicited arrival's state from the entity's preceding self-state and action. Compute *exafferent news* = arrival minus forward-model prediction, separately from history-based news.
- (c) Compute *contingency* per partner/source: how much better the arrival is predicted from the entity's immediately preceding output than from the entity's history without that output. Use a cross-predictability or transfer-entropy-like score, evaluated against shuffled pairings.
- (d) Redefine readings. Capture = high contingency *and* low exafferent news. Dull world = low news *and* low contingency. Confirmed action = low exafferent news on an elicited arrival whose forward prediction is good: report it as success, never as capture.
- (e) Make probes contingency tests. Vary the probe's content along a random direction, then measure whether the response moves along that direction (mirror) or carries independent content (world). Capture score = projection of response displacement onto probe displacement.
- (f) Tag corrections with a corollary discharge: predict their expected effect, exclude their window from news and gain baselines, and judge each correction by the residual from its prediction.

**What would show it wrong.** In a toy where the partner either echoes the entity or answers from an independent, equally low-novelty source, plus a tool that returns predictable confirmations: the news threshold alone separates all three cases at the same error rate as the contingency score. Or contingency adds no discrimination beyond news.

### 2. Gain must be split into information uptake and disturbance rejection

**Basis.** The goal statement ("pursue goals over perturbations") is a disturbance-rejection specification. A creature pursuing a goal should show *high* gain to task-relevant information and *low* gain to perturbations. Reflex gain is modulated by task phase (reflex reversal in locomotion: Forssberg et al. 1975). Sensory gating and prepulse inhibition deliberately reduce gain to irrelevant input. In motor control, optimal feedback control corrects only task-relevant deviations, the "minimum intervention principle" (Todorov and Jordan 2002). A pooled gain cannot tell an entity that is ignoring a distraction (good) from one that is ignoring its instructions (bad).

**Change in measurement.** Decompose each innovation into a component along the goal direction (origin centre toward the current task target, or into the frame's goal-relevant subspace) and an orthogonal component. Report *gain_relevant* and *gain_irrelevant* separately. "Closed loop on the entity side" becomes gain_relevant confidently below half its reference. Low gain_irrelevant is reported as healthy rejection. Self-correction is scored as the ratio gain_relevant / gain_irrelevant and by how fast the entity returns along the orthogonal directions after a planted perturbation.

**What would show it wrong.** In planted toy situations, splitting gain changes no compass reading: whenever pooled gain is low, the relevant component is also low, and planted distractors never lower pooled gain enough to cross the threshold.

### 3. Add an upper bound: the entity captured by its partner

**Basis.** Coupling between organisms runs both ways: mutual entrainment in duetting birds and synchronising fireflies, and contagion in mobbing and imitation. Echopraxia and imprinting on the wrong object are pathologies of *excess* gain. In control terms, too much loop gain produces oscillation and the loss of the controller's own reference. The design detects only "partner echoes entity". It has no reading for "entity echoes partner" (it adopts whatever arrives and its origin-ward position collapses), and currently reads that case as "news present, gain open → on track".

**Change in measurement.** Compute contingency (Finding 1c) in both directions: entity→partner and partner→entity. Add a reading: gain_relevant near 1, unexplained surprise near zero, partner→entity contingency high and falling distance-to-goal progress (or rising out-of-frame displacement toward the partner's states) = *entity captured*. Action: re-insert the origin, not probe.

**What would show it wrong.** In a toy where the entity is planted to copy the partner, existing readings already flag it (for example via drift from origin), so the new reading adds no detections. Or gain near 1 never occurs on the real model.

### 4. "Gain at zero" conflates habituation, sensory adaptation, satiation and effector limits; the reference is inflated by the orienting response

**Basis.** Habituation is stimulus-specific, shows dishabituation (a novel stimulus restores response to the old one) and spontaneous recovery after a pause (Thompson and Spencer 1966; Rankin et al. 2009). Sensory adaptation is channel-general, intensity-dependent and fast to recover. Fatigue and effector limits leave sensing intact but cap output. Satiation lowers response because the goal has been reached, the healthy end state of allostasis. The remedies differ. Pruning context suits channel-general adaptation (in a language model, attention diluted by a long context). It is the wrong remedy for stimulus-specific habituation, which calls for a novel or different source, and for satiation, where the right move is to stop. Separately, Sokolov's orienting response: the first exposures to a new setting produce enlarged, novelty-driven responses that habituate normally. A reference taken "just after an opening intake" captures that inflated orienting gain, so ordinary orienting-response habituation will cross "below half" and be flagged as closed loop.

**Change in measurement.**
- (a) Compute gain per source and per innovation direction (stimulus specificity).
- (b) When gain is low, run a dishabituation test: present an innovation along a fresh direction and check whether gain to it, and then to the old source, recovers. Recovery = habituation (switch source). No recovery for any direction = adaptation (prune). Gain intact but response bounded at a fixed magnitude whatever the innovation size = effector limit or convergence. Low gain with goal distance near zero = satiation (stop or report done).
- (c) Track spontaneous recovery after gaps.
- (d) Measure the gain reference after the orienting transient settles, e.g. the median gain over a window once gain's decay slope flattens, or calibrate the expected orienting decay curve on the real model, as D is.

**What would show it wrong.** On the real model, gain to novel directions falls together with gain to repeated sources (no stimulus specificity), and the opening gain is not higher than later healthy gain. Then the subdivision has nothing to measure.

### 5. Stereotypies respond to perturbation and then return to their cycle; measure persistence and recurrence, not just gain

**Basis.** Stereotypies are repetitive, invariant, apparently functionless behaviours (Mason 1991), typically stable attractors: a perturbation interrupts the bout briefly, then the animal resumes. Perseveration (repeating a formerly rewarded response after the contingency changes; prefrontal damage) is a different failure, in which the animal responds but along the old mapping. A gain pooled "over the following steps" will register the transient interruption and report the loop as open.

**Change in measurement.** Split response into a transient part (peak displacement) and a persistent part (displacement retained at the end of the measured response profile). Compute *persistent gain*. Add recurrence measures on the trajectory (recurrence rate, return time to previously visited states within D), and the step-vector variance. Read "entity-side loop" as persistent gain near zero plus high recurrence, even when transient gain is open. Read perseveration as response present but along the pre-change mapping: response direction correlates with responses to earlier arrivals more than with the current innovation.

**What would show it wrong.** In a toy with a planted attractor loop (perturbations decay back to a cycle), pooled gain already reads zero. Or persistent gain and recurrence add no detections over pooled gain.

### 6. Decompose unexplained surprise; admit a new axis only on consistency

**Basis.** Movement without input in animals includes central pattern generator output (structured, low-dimensional, repeatable), exploratory motor variability (songbird LMAN-driven variability, deliberately injected), vacuum activity (Lorenz: motivated behaviour without a releaser), noise, and late responses to old inputs. In this design there is also self-stimulation: the entity's own read-back words are tagged self, so they carry no innovation, and the movement they drive appears as "unexplained". That is exactly the rumination or stereotypy loop the compass is trying to catch. Learning a new latent cause in animals requires prediction errors that are *consistent* across occurrences (Rescorla and Wagner; latent-cause inference, Gershman et al. 2010), not a single large error.

**Change in measurement.** Regress each step's movement on (i) innovations (as now), (ii) the entity's own preceding outputs read back (an efference-copy term), and (iii) innovations older than the response profile. Only the residual is unexplained surprise. Report the self-output term as *self-drive*: high self-drive with low gain = self-stimulation loop. Admit a new axis only when residual directions recur consistently across separate episodes (low dimensionality, consistent direction beyond what isotropic noise of the same size would give). Isotropic residuals are noise; a large residual combined with a lost frame is "lost".

**What would show it wrong.** The self-output term explains nothing beyond innovations, and single-occurrence admission produces no more spurious axes than the consistency rule in the toy's noise-burst condition.

### 7. One 21-state horizon misses long-period loops and misreads recall as news

**Basis.** Adaptation and habituation run on several timescales at once, with power-law or multi-exponential recovery (Drew and Abbott 2006; Fairhall et al. 2001), and long-term habituation lasts days. A creature with only a short memory would treat a stimulus last seen 22 acts ago as novel.

**Change in measurement.** Compute news against at least two baselines: the last 21 states, and the whole session record (nearest prior state). Report *short news* and *long news*. Material that is novel to the window but already seen in the session is re-presentation, not news, and a stereotypy cycle longer than 21 shows up as repeatedly high short news with low long news.

**What would show it wrong.** On real transcripts, short and long news agree almost everywhere (correlation near 1 and no differing compass readings).

### 8. Probe and leave thresholds should be relative to the session's own news rate, not to D alone

**Basis.** The marginal value theorem (Charnov 1976): leave a patch when the instantaneous intake rate falls to the long-run average rate of the environment, net of travel cost. Patches deplete as they are exploited, so falling news under exploitation is expected, not a fault. Sampling rate in active sensing scales with uncertainty and closeness to the target (the echolocating bat's terminal buzz, rising whisking rate on contact).

**Change in measurement.** Maintain a running average news rate over the session and a cost per probe (tokens or steps). Probe when current news rate < long-run average − probe cost, with D kept as the floor below which news is zero. Make probe rate graded by the uncertainty of the compass reading, not a binary trigger. Score policies by news obtained per step.

**What would show it wrong.** In the toy, a fixed D-threshold policy gets equal or more news per step than the relative policy across depleting and non-depleting conditions.

### 9. The frozen origin is a homeostatic setpoint; drift should be judged against the frame, and resets weighted by accumulated uncertainty

**Basis.** Allostasis (Sterling): regulation by predictive, moving setpoints. A forager's goal state shifts as it learns where food is. Path integration in desert ants accumulates error with distance travelled, and the landmark-view correction is weighted by the integrator's uncertainty: a longer vector means more weight on views (Wystrach et al. 2015). A distance-from-origin drift measure punishes legitimate progress, and a fixed-strength origin re-insertion resets to an outdated setpoint.

**Change in measurement.** Measure drift as displacement *outside* the frame (the part of travel not along admitted, world-supplied directions), and progress as displacement within the frame. Keep the accumulated count of steps since the last admitted innovation (an uncertainty proxy), and scale correction (how much origin to re-insert, or how much to prune) with it.

**What would show it wrong.** In the toy's change-of-topic condition, distance from origin separates legitimate topic change from drift as well as out-of-frame displacement does, and fixed-strength correction recovers as fast as uncertainty-weighted correction.

### 10. The toy never reacts to the agent and is linear

**Basis.** Organism–environment coupling is closed-loop: most of an animal's input is caused by its own movement (Gibson; sensorimotor contingencies). Responses saturate and have thresholds. The toy's planted situations are open-loop arrivals apart from the echo case, so Findings 1–5 cannot show up in it, and linear gain is well defined there in a way it may not be on the real model.

**Change in measurement.** Add toy conditions where arrivals are a function of the entity's preceding action (a reactive tool with predictable confirmations, a reactive tool with independent content), a partner that is half echo and half independent, a saturating response nonlinearity, and a planted attractor loop. Report each compass reading's error rate per condition.

**What would show it wrong.** Readings in the reactive and nonlinear conditions keep the same error rates as in the current open-loop linear conditions.

### 11. D should be measured as a function of state (Weber-like), not as one number

**Basis.** Detection thresholds scale with background level (Weber) and adapt to the current input distribution (Fairhall et al. 2001). Re-reading spread may grow with context length or depend on region of activation space.

**Change in measurement.** Measure paraphrase spread at several context lengths and positions and use the local D for the news-zero cut.

**What would show it wrong.** Paraphrase spread is flat (within its own sampling error) across context length and position.

## Not actionable

- Whether a trajectory through a frozen model's activations can count as an "entity" or "organism" at all, absent a body, metabolism or stakes. Biology ties self/world distinctions to a body that can be damaged. This review treats the analogy as working terms only.
- Whether "functioning as if aware" has a biological criterion. Comparative cognition has no agreed behavioural marker for awareness (mirror marking is contested), so biology cannot supply a pass condition for the goal as stated.
- Umwelt (von Uexküll): the entity's world is limited to text, and its frame can only ever span what text conveys. True, but no measurement follows from it beyond the frame already defined.
